Google Platform is a unified internal workspace designed for teams of every size — from small squads working on a single initiative to enterprise organizations coordinating dozens of departments. It gives you a single place to manage projects, share resources, control access, and collaborate with the people you work with every day. Whether you are a project lead, a contributor, or an IT administrator, the platform adapts to the way your organization operates.

## The Workspace Model

Your organization runs inside a single, isolated **workspace**. Think of the workspace as a private container: all of your org's projects, members, resources, and settings live here and are never visible to or accessible by outside organizations. When you log in, you land directly in your workspace — there is no need to switch between tenants or manage multiple accounts.

Every piece of data scoped to your organization — member directories, project history, uploaded files, audit logs — stays within your workspace boundary. This isolation means you can collaborate freely inside the workspace without worrying that information will leak to unrelated organizations on the same platform.

## Projects

Projects are the primary unit of work in Google Platform. Each project gives your team a dedicated space to set goals, assign tasks, upload files, and track progress. You can create as many projects as your workspace needs, and you can organize them with labels or archive them when work is complete.

Every project has its own member list, so you control exactly who can see and contribute to each piece of work. A member of your workspace does not automatically have access to every project — access is granted at the project level, giving you fine-grained control over sensitive work.

## Resources

Resources are the files, links, and assets that support your work. You can attach resources directly to a project so that everything relevant to a given initiative stays in one place, or you can store resources at the workspace level to share them across multiple projects.

Common resource types include uploaded documents, images, external URLs, and data exports. Resource-level permissions let you decide whether a file is visible to all project members or restricted to a specific subset of contributors.

## Teams and Members

Members are the people in your workspace. Each member has a workspace-level role (such as Admin or Viewer) that governs what they can do across the platform, and they can also hold project-level roles that fine-tune their access within specific projects.

Teams are named groups of members that you can assign to projects all at once, making it easy to onboard an entire department or functional group without adding people one by one. Changes to a team's membership automatically propagate to every project the team belongs to.

## The Four Building Blocks

These four concepts — workspace, projects, resources, and members — work together to give your organization a structured, secure, and flexible way to get work done.
