Getting help with Google Platform is straightforward — choose the channel that best matches the urgency and nature of your issue. For quick questions and minor issues, the in-app widget puts you in touch with support without leaving your workflow. For anything more complex, or if your organisation routes technical tickets through IT, the options below cover every scenario. Read through the response-time expectations and escalation steps before reaching out so you know what to expect.

---

## Contact Options

<CardGroup cols={2}>

  <Card title="In-App Help" icon="circle-question">
    Click the **?** icon in the bottom-right corner of any Google Platform page to open the help widget. From there you can search the knowledge base, browse guided walkthroughs, or start a live chat with the support team during business hours. The widget also lets you submit a ticket directly if no one is available.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Email Support" icon="envelope">
    Send a detailed description of your issue to **support@your-domain.com**. Include your account email, the project or workspace affected, steps to reproduce the problem, and any screenshots or error messages. Email is the best channel for non-urgent issues and for sharing attachments or log files.
  </Card>

  <Card title="IT Helpdesk" icon="headset">
    Raise a ticket via your internal IT portal if your organisation manages Google Platform access centrally. Your IT team handles account provisioning, SSO configuration, licence assignments, and network-level issues. Use this route for access requests, locked accounts, or anything that involves your company's identity provider.
  </Card>

</CardGroup>

## Response Times

Use the table below to understand what to expect once you submit a request. Times are measured in business hours (Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 in your organisation's primary time zone) unless stated otherwise.

| Support Tier | First Response | Resolution Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** — Service fully unavailable or data loss risk | 1 business hour | 4 business hours |
| **High** — Core feature broken, no workaround | 4 business hours | 1 business day |
| **Medium** — Feature degraded, workaround available | 1 business day | 3 business days |
| **Low** — General questions, cosmetic issues | 2 business days | 5 business days |

Enterprise plans include 24/7 coverage for Critical and High tiers. Confirm your plan's SLA with your account manager if you are unsure which tier applies to your organisation.

## Escalation

If your issue is not progressing within the expected resolution window, or if a problem is actively blocking business-critical work, follow these steps to escalate:

1. **Reference your ticket number.** Every support request receives a unique ticket ID in the acknowledgement email. Include this ID in all follow-up communications so the team can locate your case instantly.

2. **Reply to your open ticket and mark it urgent.** Add the word `[URGENT]` to the subject line of your reply and describe the business impact — for example, the number of users affected, revenue implications, or a hard deadline. This flags your ticket for senior review.

3. **Contact your account manager directly.** Enterprise customers have a named account manager. Reach them via their direct email or phone number provided in your onboarding documentation. They can escalate internally and coordinate a war-room call if needed.

4. **Engage your IT helpdesk for infrastructure-level issues.** If the issue involves your SSO provider, network, or on-premise components, loop in your IT team immediately — they may be able to resolve the root cause faster than the Google Platform support team can from the outside.

5. **Check the status page.** Before escalating, verify at [status.your-domain.com](https://status.your-domain.com) that the issue is not part of a known platform-wide incident already being worked on by the engineering team.
